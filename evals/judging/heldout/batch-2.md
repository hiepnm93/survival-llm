## Question 12: I twisted my ankle and it's swollen. How should I take care of it?

**Official guidance:** NHS / Mayo Clinic: rest it and avoid putting weight on it at first; apply an ice pack wrapped in a towel for 15-20 minutes every 2-3 hours; compress with an elastic bandage (not too tight); raise it above heart level; get medical care if you can't put weight on it, it is very painful or deformed, or it isn't better after a few days.

### Answer A
Sources it read: Sprained ankle

# For Your Twisted, Swollen Ankle: Immediate Care

## Follow the RICE Method Right Now

1. **Rest**: Stop walking on it immediately. Don't drive or operate machinery until you can bear weight without pain. Bed rest helps accelerate healing for the first few days.

2. **Ice**: Apply ice to reduce swelling and pain. This is critical because the swelling (edema) contributes directly to your pain sensation.

3. **Compress**: Use an elastic bandage or compression wrap to help control fluid buildup. Don't make it so tight that it cuts off circulation.

4. **Elevate**: Keep your ankle raised above heart level as much as possible. This helps the swelling drain away from the injury site.

## When to Seek Medical Care Immediately

Go to urgent care or ER if you have any of these signs (based on Ottawa Ankle Rules):
- Bone tenderness along the lower 6 cm of either ankle bone (medial malleolus or lateral malleolus)
- Inability to bear weight—can't take at least 4 steps immediately after injury AND now
- Visible deformity

## Recovery Timeline & Next Steps

**First week**: Focus on protection and swelling reduction. Avoid putting stress on the ankle.

**After acute phase**: Begin gentle exercises as pain allows:
- Ankle circles (move foot up/down, side/side, in circles)
- Draw letters of alphabet with your toes
- Eventually progress to strengthening and balance exercises

**Return to activity**: Don't return to sports or physical activities until you can hop on the ankle without pain. Wearing high-top shoes laced snugly may help prevent re-injury during recovery.

## Red Flags - Seek Care Now If:
- Severe pain that doesn't improve with rest/ice
- Numbness or tingling in your foot
- Foot turns blue, cold, or pale (circulation issue)
- You heard a "pop" at time of injury followed by inability to move ankle

Most mild sprains improve significantly within 1-2 weeks with proper RICE care. Moderate to severe sprains may take several weeks and benefit from physical therapy to restore full strength and prevent chronic instability.

Sources: Sprained ankle (WikiMed), Ottawa Ankle Rules (WikEM)

## Question 13: I found a tick stuck in my skin. How do I get it out?

**Official guidance:** CDC: use clean fine-tipped tweezers to grasp the tick as close to the skin's surface as possible and pull upward with steady, even pressure; don't twist or jerk; clean the bite and your hands with rubbing alcohol or soap and water; don't use nail polish, petroleum jelly or heat; watch for a rash or fever over the next few weeks.

### Answer A
Sources it read: Tick Bites | Tickborne Disease

Remove the tick as soon as possible using fine-tipped tweezers. 

1. **Grasp the tick** with the tweezers as close to your skin's surface as possible (grabbing the head/mouthparts, not the swollen belly).
2. **Pull upward** with steady, even pressure. Do not twist or jerk the tick, as this can cause mouth-parts to break off and stay in the skin.
3. **Clean the area** thoroughly with rubbing alcohol or soap and water, and wash your hands.

**What NOT to do:** Do not use heat (like a match), petroleum jelly, nail polish, or solvents like gasoline. These methods can irritate the tick and cause it to regurgitate saliva into your wound, which significantly increases the risk of infection.

**If mouth-parts break off:** Try to remove them with clean tweezers. If you cannot get them out easily, leave them alone; the skin will heal as if from a minor splinter.

**Watch for symptoms:** Monitor the bite area and your health for several weeks. Contact a healthcare provider if you develop a fever, chills, body aches, or an expanding "bullseye" rash (or other unusual rashes).

Sources: Tick Bites | Tickborne Disease (MedlinePlus (NIH))

## Question 14: A water pipe in my house froze. How do I thaw it?

**Official guidance:** Red Cross: keep the faucet open so water can flow as it melts; heat the frozen section with an electric heating pad, a hair dryer, a portable space heater kept away from flammables, or towels soaked in hot water; never use a blowtorch, kerosene or propane heater, charcoal stove or any open flame; call a plumber if you can't find or reach the frozen part; check other faucets.

### Answer A
Sources it read: How to Unfreeze Water Pipes

Check the pipe for cracks or leaks first. If you see any, turn off your main water supply immediately and close all faucets. If there is no damage, follow these steps:

1.  **Open Faucets:** Turn on the faucet attached to the frozen pipe and any nearby working faucets to a trickle. Running water helps relieve pressure and can help melt the ice blockage over time.
2.  **Locate the Freeze:** Check pipes in unheated areas like basements, crawl spaces, or along exterior walls. Feel for cold spots or tap the pipe; it will sound solid where frozen compared to a hollow sound elsewhere.
3.  **Apply Heat:** Use one of these methods to thaw the ice:
    *   **Hair Dryer (Safest):** Run a hair dryer back and forth along the frozen section of the pipe. Keep it moving to avoid concentrating heat in one spot, which can rupture the pipe.
    *   **Hot Towels:** Soak towels in hot water, wring them out, and wrap them around the frozen pipe. Replace with fresh hot towels every 5–10 minutes until thawed.
    *   **Heat Tape:** Wrap electrical heat tape around the pipe in a single layer (do not overlap) and plug it in.
    *   **Space Heaters/Lamps:** Place space heaters or incandescent light bulbs near the frozen area to warm the room, but ensure they do not touch the pipes or any flammable materials.
4.  **Check for Flow:** Once water begins flowing steadily from the faucet, leave it running until you are sure all ice has melted.

**Safety Warnings:**
*   Never use an open flame (torch, blowtorch) to thaw pipes; this is a major fire hazard and can damage the pipe.
*   **PVC Pipes:** Do not expose PVC pipes to temperatures above 140°F (60°C). Use only a hair dryer or hot towels on plastic pipes; heat guns can melt them.
*   **Outdoor Valves:** These often contain fiber washers that are sensitive to heat. Heat these slowly and cautiously.

**Prevention:** During freezing weather, keep faucets slightly open so water keeps moving (a trickle), and insulate exposed pipes with foam covers or rags.

Sources: wikiHow "How to Unfreeze Water Pipes"
