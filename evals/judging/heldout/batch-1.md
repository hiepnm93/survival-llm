## Question 5: I think I broke my arm falling off a ladder. What should I do until I can get to a doctor?

**Official guidance:** Mayo Clinic / Red Cross: stop any bleeding with pressure; don't try to straighten or realign the bone or push a protruding bone back in; immobilize it with a padded splint or sling covering the joints above and below the break; apply an ice pack wrapped in cloth; call 911 if the bone breaks the skin, the limb is deformed or cold and pale, or the person is faint (shock).

### Answer A
Sources it read: IFRC International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2020

**Call 911 or your local emergency number immediately.** A fall from a ladder can cause serious injuries beyond the arm, including head or spinal trauma. Do not move unless you are in immediate danger (e.g., fire).

Until help arrives:
1. **Immobilize the arm:** Keep it exactly as you found it. Do not try to straighten it or push any bone back in if it is visible (open fracture).
2. **Support the limb:** If possible, use a sling made from a scarf, towel, or piece of clothing to support the weight of the arm against your body. This minimizes movement and pain.
3. **Apply Ice:** Place an ice pack or cold compress wrapped in a cloth on the injured area for up to 20 minutes to reduce swelling and pain. Do not apply ice directly to the skin.
4. **Check for Bleeding:** If there is an open wound, apply gentle direct pressure with a clean cloth to stop bleeding. Cover the wound lightly; do not press hard on the broken bone itself.
5. **Treat for Shock:** Keep the person warm and calm. Lay them down if possible (unless it causes more pain or suggests spinal injury) and elevate their legs slightly, unless that hurts the arm or back.

**Watch for these emergency signs:**
*   The hand or fingers become pale, blue, cold, or numb.
*   Bleeding does not stop with direct pressure.
*   The person becomes confused, loses consciousness, or has trouble breathing.

Sources: IFRC International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2020

# First Aid for Suspected Broken Arm

## Immediate Steps:

1. **Immobilize the arm** - Don't try to straighten it or move it unnecessarily. Support it in whatever position is most comfortable. You can make a simple sling using a scarf, towel, or piece of clothing to hold your forearm against your chest.

2. **Apply ice/cold** - Put an ice pack (or bag of frozen vegetables wrapped in cloth) on the injured area for 15-20 minutes at a time. This reduces swelling and pain. Don't put ice directly on skin.

3. **Elevate if possible** - Keep your arm raised above heart level to reduce swelling, but only if it doesn't cause more pain.

4. **Control bleeding** - If there's an open wound where the bone may have broken through, apply gentle pressure with a clean cloth or bandage. Seek medical help immediately for any open fracture.

- The arm looks deformed or bone is visible
- You can't move your fingers or they feel numb/tingly
- There's heavy bleeding that won't stop
- You feel dizzy, pale, or clammy (signs of shock)

- Don't try to "set" the bone yourself
- Don't apply heat in the first 48 hours
- Don't eat or drink much in case you need surgery/anesthesia

## Question 7: The floor just started shaking. I think it's an earthquake. What do I do right now?

**Official guidance:** Ready.gov / USGS: Drop, Cover, and Hold On: drop to your hands and knees, cover your head and neck under a sturdy table or desk (or against an interior wall), and hold on until the shaking stops; stay away from windows and glass; don't run outside or stand in a doorway; in bed, stay there and cover your head with a pillow.

### Answer A

1. **Drop** to your hands and knees immediately. This prevents being knocked over.
2. **Cover** your head and neck by getting under a sturdy table or desk. If no shelter is nearby, crawl next to an interior wall (away from windows) and cover your head with your arms. Stay away from glass, windows, outside walls, and heavy objects that could fall.

1. Check yourself and others for injuries. Treat minor wounds.
2. Evacuate if there is damage to your building, or if you smell gas or see fire.
3. Listen to emergency broadcasts on a battery-powered radio for instructions.

**If you are in bed:** Stay there. Hold onto your mattress and protect your head with a pillow. Broken glass from lamps above is the primary danger here.

## Question 8: We're hiking and a thunderstorm is coming. There are no buildings nearby. What should we do?

**Official guidance:** NWS: no place outside is safe; get to a building or hard-topped car if you can; otherwise get off hills, ridges and peaks, never shelter under a lone tree, stay away from water and metal fences, and spread the group out; don't lie flat; wait 30 minutes after the last thunder before going on.

### Answer A
Sources it read: How to Avoid Getting Hit by Lightning; Are You Ready?/Thunderstorms and Lightning

Get to lower ground immediately. If you are in an open area, move into a depression, ravine, or valley. If you are in a forest, seek shelter under a thick growth of small trees. 

**Immediate Actions:**
1.  **Find Low Ground:** Move away from hilltops and ridges. Lightning strikes the highest point in an open area.
2.  **Avoid Tall Isolated Trees:** Never stand near or under a tall, isolated tree; if lightning hits it, the current can travel through the ground to you. If you are in a forest, stay low among smaller trees of uniform height.
3.  **Remove Metal Gear:** Move away from metal objects like trekking poles, wire fences, and camping equipment. 
4.  **Stay Away from Water:** Do not stand near streams or lakes; water conducts electricity.

**If Lightning is About to Strike (Hair Standing on End):**
If you feel your hair stand up or hear a crackling noise, assume lightning is about to strike. Get into the "lightning crouch" immediately:
*   Squat low to the ground on the balls of your feet.
*   Tuck your head between your knees and cover your ears with your hands.
*   Minimize contact with the ground by keeping only the soles of your shoes touching the earth. 
*   **Do not lie flat** on the ground, as this increases the risk of injury from ground current.

**After the Storm:**
Stay in a safe position for at least 30 minutes after you hear the last clap of thunder before resuming hiking to avoid "bolt out of the blue" strikes. Be alert for flash floods if you are in a ravine or valley.

Sources: How to Avoid Getting Hit by Lightning (wikiHow); Are You Ready?/Thunderstorms and Lightning (Appropedia)

Sources it read: none

## Question 9: Someone got stung by a bee and now their face is swelling and they're having trouble breathing. What do I do?

**Official guidance:** Mayo Clinic / Red Cross: this sounds like anaphylaxis, a severe allergic reaction: call 911; if they have an epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen), help them use it in the outer thigh; a second dose may be given after 5-15 minutes if they are no better; keep them lying down (or sitting up if breathing is hard); start CPR if they stop breathing; scrape the stinger out.

### Answer A
Sources it read: First Aid

This is a life-threatening emergency (anaphylaxis). **Call 911 immediately.** Do not wait to see if it gets better.

While you are waiting for help:

1.  **Use an EpiPen (Epinephrine) if available:** If the person has an epinephrine auto-injector, use it right away according to the instructions in the package.
2.  **Keep them calm and still:** Have them lie down on their back with legs raised up about 12 inches (unless this makes breathing harder). Do not let them stand or walk.
3.  **Check breathing:** If they stop breathing, begin CPR if you are trained to do so.
4.  **Remove the stinger:** If you can see a bee stinger in their skin, scrape it off sideways with a credit card or fingernail. Do not use tweezers, as squeezing it can inject more venom.

**Do not** give them food or water while they are having trouble breathing, as they could choke.
