## Question 20: How can I tell if home-canned food might have botulism?

**Official guidance:** Possible botulism: bulging, leaking or swollen containers, spurting liquid, foam, cloudy liquid, off smell. Never taste; discard safely (sealed bags), clean spills with bleach; get medical care for symptoms.

### Answer A
Sources it read: Botulism; USDA Home Canning Guide 1: Principles of Home Canning

You cannot reliably detect botulism toxin by taste or smell—botulinum toxin is odorless and tasteless. However, you can identify jars that *might* be contaminated by looking for these warning signs:

**Visual Signs:**
1. **Bulging or swollen lids**—gas from bacterial growth pressurizes the jar, causing the lid to dome outward
2. **Leaking containers**—liquid seeping out indicates compromised seals and possible contamination
3. **Spurting liquid when opened**—pressure buildup causes food/liquid to spray out
4. **Unnatural colors** in the food compared to normal appearance
5. **Rising air bubbles** within the jar (not just trapped during filling)
6. **Mold growth**—white, blue, black, or green cotton-like patches on the food surface or underside of the lid

**Other Warning Signs:**
7. **Unsealed lids**—a properly sealed jar has a concave (dented) center; if it pops when pressed, the seal failed
8. **Unusual odors** when opening—though some contaminated jars may smell normal

**Critical Safety Rules:**
- **Never taste suspect food to "check" if it's safe**—even a tiny amount of botulinum toxin can be fatal
- **Low-acid foods are highest risk**: vegetables, meats, fish, and tomatoes (unless properly acidified) are more likely to support botulism growth than high-acid fruits
- If you suspect contamination: wear gloves, place sealed jars in heavy garbage bags before disposal, or boil unsealed jars for 30 minutes first to destroy any toxin before opening

**If someone develops symptoms** (double vision, difficulty swallowing, muscle weakness, paralysis)—usually within 12-36 hours after eating contaminated food—this is a **medical emergency. Call 911 immediately.** Antitoxin must be given quickly; botulism can cause respiratory failure and death.

### Answer B
Sources it read: USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning: Introduction

You often cannot tell if home-canned food contains botulism just by looking at or smelling it, as the bacteria and its toxin are odorless and tasteless. However, you can identify jars that are likely unsafe based on their appearance and behavior when opened.

**Warning Signs of Contamination:**
1.  **Bulging or Swollen Lids:** If the lid is convex (pushed outward) rather than concave (sucked down), gas has been produced inside. This is a major red flag.
2.  **Spurting Liquid:** When you open the jar, if liquid spurts out, discard it immediately.
3.  **Unusual Odors:** While botulism itself may not smell, other spoilage bacteria often produce foul or "off" odors. If it smells bad, do not eat it.

*   **Check Your Method:** Botulism is most common in low-acid foods (vegetables, meats, poultry) that were canned using a boiling water bath instead of a pressure canner. If you used the wrong method for the food type, assume it is unsafe regardless of how it looks.
*   **When in Doubt, Throw It Out:** There is no reliable way to "fix" contaminated food at home other than heat.
