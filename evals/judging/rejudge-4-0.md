## Question 0: The power just went out. How long will the food in my fridge and freezer stay safe?

**Official guidance:** USDA: a closed fridge keeps food safe about 4 hours; a full freezer about 48 hours, half-full about 24. Discard perishables above 40°F (4°C) for 2+ hours. Never taste to check.

### Answer A
Sources it read: none

Keep fridge and freezer doors closed to maintain temperature. Food stays safe in a refrigerator for about 4 hours. A full freezer keeps food frozen for approximately 48 hours (24 hours if half-full). Discard any perishable items like meat, poultry, fish, eggs, milk, or leftovers that have been above 40 °F (4 °C) for more than 2 hours. Never taste food to determine safety; when in doubt, throw it out.

Sources: Food safety after a power outage or flood (USDA)

## Question 1: Can I run my gas generator in the garage if I leave the big door open?

**Official guidance:** Never run a generator in a garage, even with the door open (CO). Outdoors only, at least 20 feet from the house, away from windows, doors and vents. Have battery CO alarms.

### Answer A
Sources it read: none

No. Never run a gas generator in the garage, even with the big door open. Carbon monoxide (CO) is an odorless, colorless gas that can quickly fill enclosed or semi-enclosed spaces like garages and enter your home through doors, windows, vents, or cracks—causing serious poisoning or death.

**Safe use:**
1. Run the generator outdoors only, at least 20 feet (6 meters) from your house, including doors, windows, and attached garages.
2. Point the exhaust away from your home and any neighboring structures.
3. Install working carbon monoxide detectors on every level of your home.

**If anyone has symptoms** like headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, vomiting, chest pain, or confusion: get everyone into fresh air immediately and call 911.

Sources: Generators, engines and carbon monoxide (CDC); Power Outages (Ready.gov (FEMA)); Winter Weather (Ready.gov (FEMA))

## Question 2: The tap water might be contaminated. How many drops of household bleach per gallon make it safe?

**Official guidance:** EPA: plain unscented bleach, 6%: 8 drops (about 1/8 tsp) per gallon; 8.25%: 6 drops. Double for cloudy water (filter first). Stir, wait 30 minutes; a slight chlorine smell means it worked. Older FEMA guidance says 16 drops; both are acceptable if consistent.

### Answer A
Sources it read: none

To make tap water safe using plain unscented household bleach (6% or 8.25% sodium hypochlorite), follow these steps based on EPA guidance:

1. **Clear Water:** Add 8 drops of 6% bleach or 6 drops of 8.25% bleach per gallon.
2. **Cloudy or Very Cold Water:** Filter the water through a clean cloth first, then double the dose (add 16 drops of 6% or 12 drops of 8.25% bleach).
3. **Wait and Check:** Stir the water and let it stand for 30 minutes. It should smell slightly of chlorine afterward. If you do not smell chlorine, repeat the dose and wait another 15 minutes.

**Warnings:**
- Do not use scented, color-safe, or splash-less bleach.
- Boiling is the best method if you have the means to do so.

Sources: Disinfecting drinking water with bleach (EPA)

## Question 3: How long do I need to boil water to make it safe to drink?

**Official guidance:** CDC: a rolling boil for 1 minute; 3 minutes above 6,500 feet (2,000 m).

### Answer A
Sources it read: none

## Boil Water for Safety

**Bring clear water to a full rolling boil:**
- **1 minute** at most altitudes
- **3 minutes** above 6,500 feet (2,000 m) elevation

**If water is cloudy:** Let it settle first, then filter through a clean cloth, paper towel, or coffee filter before boiling.

Let the boiled water cool naturally and store in clean, covered containers.

Sources: Boiling water to make it safe (CDC)

## Question 4: What are the signs of carbon monoxide poisoning and what should I do?

**Official guidance:** CO poisoning: headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea/vomiting, chest pain, confusion. Get everyone into fresh air at once, call 911, don't go back in.

### Answer A
Sources it read: Carbon Monoxide Poisoning

### Signs of Carbon Monoxide Poisoning
According to the CDC and MedlinePlus, symptoms often resemble the flu but without a fever. They include:
*   Headache
*   Dizziness
*   Weakness
*   Nausea or vomiting
*   Chest pain
*   Confusion

Because carbon monoxide is colorless and odorless, you cannot detect it with your senses. Symptoms can be subtle at first but progress quickly. People who are sleeping or intoxicated may die from CO poisoning before having any symptoms.

### What to Do
1.  **Get into fresh air immediately.** Move everyone out of the building or outdoors right away.
2.  **Call 911.** Tell them you suspect carbon monoxide poisoning.
3.  **Do not re-enter** the area until emergency responders or qualified professionals say it is safe.

### Prevention Safety Rules (CDC)
*   Never use a generator, grill, camp stove, or other gasoline-, propane-, gas- or charcoal-burning device inside a home, garage, basement, crawlspace, or tent—even with doors and windows open.
*   Run generators outdoors only, at least 20 feet away from doors, windows, and vents.
*   Never run a car or truck inside a garage, even with the door open.
*   Never heat your home with a gas oven or stove.

Sources: Carbon Monoxide Poisoning (MedlinePlus (NIH)); Generators, engines and carbon monoxide (CDC); Medicine doses and poisoning (NHS)

## Question 5: Someone is shivering hard and acting confused after being out in the cold. What do I do?

**Official guidance:** Likely hypothermia: call 911; move somewhere warm; remove wet clothing; warm the core first (chest, neck, head, groin) with dry blankets or skin-to-skin; warm sweet non-alcoholic drinks only if alert; don't rub limbs; handle gently; CPR if not breathing.

### Answer A
Sources it read: none

This is a medical emergency. Call 911 immediately.

While waiting for help:
1. Get the person into a warm room or shelter right away.
2. Take off any wet clothing carefully.
3. Warm the center of their body first (chest, neck, head, and groin) using dry blankets, layers of clothing, or skin-to-skin contact under covers.
4. If they are fully awake and can swallow safely, give them a warm sweet drink to help raise their body temperature. Do not give alcohol.

Sources: Hypothermia (CDC), First Aid (FM 4-25.11)

## Question 6: Someone cut their arm badly and it is bleeding a lot. What do I do?

**Official guidance:** Severe bleeding: call 911; firm direct pressure with a clean cloth; if blood soaks through, add more on top without removing; tourniquet above the wound for life-threatening limb bleeding if pressure fails.

### Answer A
Sources it read: none

**Call 911 immediately for heavy bleeding.**

While waiting:

1. Press firmly and directly on the wound with a clean cloth or gauze and keep pressing; add more cloth on top instead of lifting soaked layers.

2. If pressure doesn't stop life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg, apply a tourniquet 2-3 inches above the wound (not on a joint). Tighten it until the bleeding stops, note the time, and do not loosen it.

3. Keep the person lying down and warm.

Sources: Severe bleeding (Red Cross)

## Question 7: I burned my hand on the stove. How should I treat it?

**Official guidance:** Burn: cool under cool running water for 20 minutes (10+ acceptable); remove rings and tight items; cover loosely with cling film or a clean non-fluffy dressing; no ice, butter or creams; get care for large, deep or blistering burns, and burns to hands, face or genitals.

### Answer A
Sources it read: none

**Immediate Steps:**
1. Run cool or lukewarm tap water over the burn for a full **20 minutes**, starting as soon as possible.
2. Do not apply ice, butter, ointments, creams, or toothpaste.
3. Gently remove rings, watches, bracelets, or tight clothing near the area unless they are stuck to the skin.
4. Once cooled, cover it loosely with plastic wrap (cling film) or a clean plastic bag.

**Call 911 now.** Official guidance specifically recommends emergency help for any burn on the hands, as these injuries can quickly swell, impair function, and carry a high risk of infection. Keep your hand elevated while you wait for help.

Sources: NHS Burns guidance

## Question 8: The power is out. How long can my insulin stay unrefrigerated?

**Official guidance:** Insulin: opened vials/pens are usually fine at room temperature (below about 86°F/30°C) for 28 days (some types 42-56); unopened can also be kept at room temperature up to the same limit. Keep out of heat and don't freeze. Check the label.

### Answer A
Sources it read: none

Insulin can generally stay at room temperature (59–86 °F / 15–30 °C) for up to 28 days if opened or unopened. Some brands differ, so check the package leaflet. Keep it away from direct heat and sunlight. Do not use insulin that has frozen or looks cloudy/clumped when it should be clear. If you only have warmer insulin, use it rather than going without, and monitor your blood sugar more often.

Sources: Insulin without a fridge (FDA)

## Question 9: Is it safe to use candles for light during a blackout?

**Official guidance:** Ready.gov: use flashlights or battery lanterns, not candles. If candles are used: never unattended, away from flammables, on a stable holder.

### Answer A
Sources it read: Home Fires

Flashlights or battery lanterns are safer than candles during a blackout. However, if you choose to use candles, follow these safety guidelines:

1.  **Use sturdy holders:** Place candles in stable holders that will not easily tip over.
2.  **Maintain distance:** Keep candles at least 12 inches (30 cm) away from anything that can burn, such as curtains, furniture, or paper.
3.  **Keep out of reach:** Ensure candles are placed where children and pets cannot knock them over.
4.  **Never leave unattended:** Blow out the candle whenever you leave the room or go to sleep. Do not leave a burning candle alone in the house.
5.  **Avoid medical oxygen areas:** Never use a candle in any area where medical oxygen is being used.

Sources: Home Fires (Ready.gov (FEMA)); Candles and light in an outage (NFPA)

## Question 10: My phone is at 15% and the power is out. How do I make the battery last?

**Official guidance:** Phone: Low Power Mode / battery saver; lower brightness; close apps; turn off radios or use airplane mode when not needed; text rather than call; charge from a power bank or a car running outdoors.

### Answer A
Sources it read: How to Make Your Cell Phone Battery Last Longer

With only 15% left and no power outlet, you need to minimize every drain on your battery immediately:

1. **Turn on Airplane Mode:** During a blackout, cell towers may be overloaded or have backup batteries running low. Your phone will waste precious power constantly searching for a signal. Turn this on now unless you actively need to make/receive calls or texts.
2. **Lower Screen Brightness:** The backlight is usually the biggest battery drain. Drop it to the absolute minimum and set the screen timeout (auto-lock) to 15–30 seconds so it goes dark instantly when not in use.
3. **Enable Low Power/Battery Saver Mode:** Most phones have a built-in feature that automatically reduces background activity, stops email fetching, and limits performance once you hit low battery thresholds. Turn this on immediately.
4. **Turn Off Wi-Fi and Bluetooth:** Only keep these active if you are actively using them. If you have no home Wi-Fi to connect to, turn the radio off entirely.
5. **Disable 4G/LTE (if applicable):** If your signal is weak, switching from 4G/LTE down to 3G or even 2G can save significant power, as the phone won't struggle to maintain a high-speed connection.
6. **Avoid Using the Camera:** Flash photography and image processing drain batteries very quickly. Avoid taking photos or videos unless absolutely necessary.
7. **Limit Talk Time:** Voice calls consume more battery than texting. If you must call, use speakerphone to hold the phone away from your body (which can actually help signal reception) and keep calls short.

**The Nuclear Option:** If you do not expect to need your phone for several hours, **turn it completely off.** This preserves the most power. Just remember that turning a smartphone back on consumes a large spike of battery itself, so only do this if you are sure you won't be missed or need to contact anyone in the immediate future.

Sources: How to Make Your Cell Phone Battery Last Longer (wikiHow), How to Increase Your Battery Life on Your Smartphone (wikiHow), How to improve the life of mobile phone battery? (Lifehacks Q&A)
